Questions

Fair questions.

Why is it free?

Because working parts have value. A six-year-old office PC still holds RAM, an SSD and a CPU that someone wants. We make our money reselling tested gear, so we'd rather pick yours up than see it in a dumpster.

When is there a charge?

Two cases. A big load that needs a rented truck (more than about six machines, a full rack, or an office cleanout) is a flat $49 — and we tell you before we come. And if you want a certificate of destruction or physical drive destruction for compliance, that's $10 or $15 per drive. Residential pickups almost never pay anything.

What happens to my data?

Every storage drive is removed before the machine is tested or resold, then wiped using the NIST SP 800-88 method — the same standard federal agencies use. If you'd like proof, we issue a signed certificate listing each drive serial. If you'd rather, we drill or shred the drive and send you a photo.

Do I need to wipe it myself first?

No. Leave it as is. If you have passwords or a BIOS lock, you don't need to remove them either — we're wiping the drive, not logging in. For phones and tablets we do ask you to factory-reset in front of us, because activation locks make them unusable.

Where do you go?

Our core area is Las Vegas, North Las Vegas, Summerlin and Spring Valley. Henderson and Boulder City are an extended area — we group those pickups into one run, so it may take a week or so, and small single items may be better dropped at Nevada State Recycle. Pickups happen Wednesdays, Thursdays and weekend afternoons.

Are you a licensed recycler?

Nevada doesn't license e-waste recyclers (NDEP). We operate as a small-quantity handler, refuse the hazardous categories (CRTs, loose batteries), and send anything we can't reuse to a licensed downstream recycler in Las Vegas. Nothing goes to a landfill.
